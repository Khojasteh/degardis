## What a source is

A Degardis source is a knowledge base. You write what your skill knows as small
Markdown files, say which of them each kind of work needs, and the compiler
assembles a page per kind of work with its complete knowledge closure. Principles
and guides remain separately loaded pages: a skill or task names a principle,
and a task names a guide.

Five constructs make up a source:

- A **task** is a recognizable class of work someone asks for — summarizing
  supplied material, reviewing a draft, investigating a complaint. A request is
  routed to a task.
- A **knowledge unit** is one reusable piece of what the skill knows. Its `kind`
  says whether it is a concept, fact, constraint, or guidance. Every task that
  needs it gets it.
- A **principle** is guidance that holds across several of your tasks rather
  than being about the subject matter — keeping observation apart from
  inference, what a report owes its reader. Skills and tasks name the ones they
  want.
- A **guide** is a separately loaded detail page. Tasks name the ones they
  need.
- A **profile** is situational: who the audience is, what kind of material is in
  hand, which jurisdiction applies. It applies because of what the agent is
  looking at, not because of which task it is doing.

All five constructs are yours; the compiler supplies no domain text of its own.

What it decides is placement, never wording. It moves your material, groups
knowledge by its declared kind, adjusts heading levels so a document becomes a
section, and re-addresses relative links so they still resolve. It never
paraphrases, merges, summarizes, reorders, or drops any of it. A page that comes
out over budget is reported with what weighs most on it, and what to cut is
yours.

Your file structure is compiled away: a task page carries the whole closure of
what its task needs. What the bundle keeps is the decisions only the running
agent can make — which task a request is, which profiles the situation calls
for, and whether a principle or guide activation applies. Omitted activation
applies unconditionally.
