## What a source is

A Degardis source is authored material plus declarations of which work needs it.
The compiler turns that source into an agent skill with one page per task.

Five constructs make up a source:

- A **task** is a recognizable class of requested work. A request is routed to
  one task.
- A **knowledge unit** is one reusable piece of what the skill knows. Its `kind`
  classifies it as a concept, fact, constraint, or guidance.
- A **principle** is working guidance shared across kinds of work.
- A **guide** is detail a task or a facet loads as a separate page.
- A **facet** is guidance selected by one aspect of the situation rather than the task.

All five constructs are yours; the compiler supplies no domain text of its own.

The compiler decides placement, never wording. It may re-level headings,
re-address relative links, and fold hard-wrapped prose; it does not paraphrase,
merge, summarize, reorder, or drop authored material.

Each task page carries its complete knowledge closure. Principles and guides
remain separate because their activation may decide whether they are read;
facets remain separate because the situation selects them. Omitted activation
means unconditional.
