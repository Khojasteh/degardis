---
title: Reading a transcript in two passes
---

A transcript is read twice because the two readings answer different questions, and trying to answer both at once is what produces a summary that records the argument instead of the outcome.

## First pass: what was discussed

Read the whole transcript without writing a summary line. Note only the subjects, in the order they come up, and who spoke to each one. A subject that is raised, dropped, and raised again is one subject.

## Second pass: where each subject ended

Take the subjects one at a time and read only the passages belonging to each. For every subject, record:

- the last position each speaker held, and the passage it came from,
- whether the group settled it, left it open, or deferred it,
- what was decided in passing, which is usually stated once and briefly.

Where a speaker's position changed, carry the last one. Carry the earlier one only where the change is itself what the reader needs to know — a reversal after new information, or a concession that explains the decision.

## What neither pass produces

Neither pass resolves a disagreement the transcript leaves open. Record it as open, with each position attributed, and say what would settle it.
