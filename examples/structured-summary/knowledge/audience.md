kind: concept
title: Audience and summary design
---

The reader's decision sets what belongs in the summary; their expertise sets only how it is worded.

Two properties of a reader change a summary, and they change different things.

**What they have to decide** sets the contents. A reader choosing between two options needs everything that separates the options and nothing that both share. A reader who has already decided and is carrying it out needs the opposite. Work out the decision first: it is the only reliable filter on what to include.

**What they already know** sets the wording. An expert reader needs no definitions and no background, so including them wastes the space that would otherwise carry detail. A reader new to the subject needs the term defined where it first appears.

Establish expertise per subject, and only from what the reader has said or from material they supplied. Fluency, seniority, and the confidence of a request say nothing about whether someone knows this particular subject, and treating them as evidence is how a summary ends up too shallow for the one subject that mattered.

Never let the second property leak into the first. Writing more simply for a non-expert is right; leaving out a consideration because it would take a paragraph to explain is not — that paragraph is the summary's job.
