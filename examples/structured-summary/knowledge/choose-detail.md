---
kind: guidance
title: Decision-relevant detail
requires:
- audience
---

Cut to the shortest text that keeps every distinction the reader has to act on.

Length is not the target. The target is that nothing the reader must distinguish
has been collapsed, and nothing else survives.

Work from the decision backwards:

1. List what the reader has to be able to tell apart — two options, a risk and
   its mitigation, a claim and its qualifier.
2. Keep every sentence that carries one of those distinctions.
3. Cut everything else, including material that is interesting, well written, or
   hard-won.

The usual failure is not length but flattening: three positions become "the team
discussed options", and the reader now has less than they started with. If a
sentence removes a distinction rather than a word, it is not a shorter summary,
it is a different one.

Where you genuinely cannot tell whether a distinction matters, keep it and say
in one clause why it might. That costs the reader a line. Cutting it costs them
the decision.
