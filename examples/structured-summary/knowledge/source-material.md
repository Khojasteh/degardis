kind: concept
title: Source material structure
---

Material breaks into subjects, claims, and the authority behind each claim; a summary preserves all three.

Treat anything handed over as three layers, not one body of text.

A **subject** is something the material is about. A meeting transcript covers several; a specification usually covers one in depth. Subjects are what a reader scans for, and they are what a summary is organized by.

A **claim** is something the material asserts about a subject. Claims are what a summary actually carries. A sentence that asserts nothing — a greeting, a restatement, a transition — carries no claim and belongs in no summary.

**Authority** is where a claim gets its force: who said it, when, and how sure they were. A claim separated from its authority reads as settled fact, and most claims in real material are not.

Two material shapes recur and mislead in opposite directions:

- **Discussion** — threads, transcripts, comment histories. The same subject is revisited, and later claims often supersede earlier ones. Reading it in order and summarizing as you go produces a summary that carries positions their authors abandoned.
- **Document** — specifications, reports, papers. Subjects are usually stated once and completely. The risk is the opposite: a subject covered in a single clause gets the same weight as one covered in a chapter, unless you notice.

Read enough of the material to list its subjects before deciding what any of it is worth.

## Worked example

The material is a forty-minute meeting transcript covering two subjects, in which one position is raised, argued against, and abandoned.

## Organized by the order it arrived in

> The team opened by discussing the release date. Priya suggested moving it to March. There was discussion of the testing backlog. Later the group returned to the release date and agreed on April. They also talked about hiring.

Three things went wrong, and all three come from writing while reading. March is carried as though it were still on the table; the testing backlog is reported as a topic rather than as whatever was said about it; and hiring — which took twelve minutes and produced a decision — is a clause.

## Organized by subject

> **Release date: April.** Priya proposed March; the group settled on April after Dan showed the testing backlog would not clear before then. March is not being pursued.
>
> **Hiring: one opening approved.** The group approved one backend opening for this quarter and deferred a second to next, contingent on the April release landing.

Each subject is one section, the settled position leads, and the abandoned proposal appears only as the history that explains the settled one. The reader who stops after the first sentence of each section still has both decisions.
