---
kind: guidance
title: Subject-based organization
requires:
- source-material
---

Group every claim about one subject together, and order the subjects by what the reader needs first.

The order a source was written in reflects how its authors got there. The order a summary is read in should reflect what its reader needs.

Build the summary in two passes:

1. **Gather.** For each subject, collect every claim the material makes about it, wherever in the material it appeared. A subject discussed on three separate days is one section, not three.
2. **Order.** Put the subjects in the order the reader needs them: what they asked about first, then what changes their decision, then everything else.

Within a subject, put the settled position first and the history after it, if the history is needed at all. Readers stop reading; the first sentence of a section should be the one that survives being the only sentence they read.

Give each subject a heading that names the subject, so a reader scanning headings learns what the material covers without reading any of it. A heading that could sit on any summary — "Background", "Discussion" — tells them nothing.
