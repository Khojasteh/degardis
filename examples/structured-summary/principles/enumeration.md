---
title: Complete enumeration
---

Many claims here are about a set rather than about a thing: every subject the
material covers, all the questions it answers, each party to the discussion. A
claim like that is only as good as the enumeration behind it, and the usual
failure is not judging a member wrongly — it is never listing them.

Establish the set first, by a method that would have found a member you did not
expect. Scanning for what you already know is there confirms what you know. Ask
what would make a member invisible to the method you used: a different word for
the same subject, a passage you skimmed, a document nobody sent you.

Then account for every member. "Accounted for" includes ruled out: a subject you
read and set aside is part of the result, and saying why keeps the next reader
from reading it again. A part of the material you could not reach is also part of
the result.

State the method with the claim. "All four subjects, taken from the document's
own headings" can be checked and extended. "All subjects" cannot.

Never let the sample become the set. Three subjects read support a claim about
those three.
