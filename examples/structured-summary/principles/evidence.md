---
title: Claim status
activation: Before writing a claim that is not directly stated in the material
---

Give every claim in the result one of four statuses: the material stated it, you inferred it from something the material stated, you assumed it, or it remains unknown.

Sort each one as you write it:

- **Stated.** The material says it. Name where, so a reader can check.
- **Inferred.** It follows from something stated. Give what it follows from; an inference whose basis is not shown cannot be disagreed with.
- **Assumed.** You needed it to write the result and could not establish it. Mark it where it is used, not only where you made it.
- **Unknown.** You could not establish it and did not need to assume it. Say so. An absent claim reads as a claim that there was nothing to say.

An assumption that goes unmarked becomes a stated fact in the next reader's hands, and no later step recovers the difference.

Do not raise a claim's category to make the result read better. A hedged sentence about something the material stated is worth more than a confident one about something you supplied.
