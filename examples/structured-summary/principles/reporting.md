---
title: Reader-first reporting
---

The requester wants the shorter text and what it costs them to act on it. They did not ask to watch you read.

Lead with the result: what the material settles, what you produced, what you concluded. Then give what the reader needs to trust or act on it.

Leave out the process. Which files you opened, which order you read them in, how long it took, and which approach you abandoned are yours, not theirs.

Never leave out a limit. Everything that qualifies the result goes in, at the same standard however short the material was:

- what the material does not settle,
- where the result rests on an assumption,
- where two parts of the material disagree,
- anything that would change the conclusion if it turned out otherwise.

A limit buried under a summary of your effort has not been reported. Put it beside the claim it qualifies.

Say plainly when the material does not support what was asked for. That is a useful result; the same gap smoothed over is not, and the requester has no way to tell the two apart.
